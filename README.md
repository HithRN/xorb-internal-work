# Project Cardiac Diagnostics: Physiology-Informed Edge AI

Welcome to the **Cardiac Diagnostics** project. This repository is a secure, private environment for PhD researchers.   
We are pushing the boundaries of medical Edge-AI by moving away from purely data-driven models, instead embedding **cardiac physiology and anatomy** directly into hardware-constrained neural networks.

## Core Hardware Target
**Platform:** Analog Devices MAX78002 
**Constraints:** 1.3 MB Flash/SRAM, 64x 8-bit Parallel Convolutional Accelerators 
* **The Challenge:** Standard QAT (Quantization-Aware Training) drops our datacenter accuracy from 99.0% down to an unacceptable 90-91% on-chip due to 8-bit quantization noise on raw ECG signals.

## Our Research Paradigm: The Physiology-Informed Leap
To achieve clinical-grade accuracy (>99%) within a 1.3 MB footprint, this project is divided into two distinct, interconnected phases based on the **Projektplan-LTU.docx**:

### 🚀 Phase 1: Adaptive Waveform Morphing & Residual Classification (Project 1)
Instead of feeding raw, noisy ECG signals into the MAX78002's CNN:
1. **Calibration Gate:** The chip runs a lightweight, deterministic calibration loop (e.g., using parametric ODEs like the McSharry model) during the first minutes to learn the patient's unique, stable baseline (even if deformed by old myocardial infarctions).
2. **Residual Subtraction:** We subtract this adaptive physiological model from the live ECG.
3. **8-bit Residual CNN:** The CNN *only* processes the **residual error**. Normal beats become a flat zero line, making pathological beats (A-fib, PVCs, Asystole) stand out with high amplitude. This shields the network from quantization noise and slashes the required model size from megabytes to kilobytes.

### 🧠 Phase 2: Cardiomechanical Wave Coupling (Project 2 - Sensor Fusion)
To eliminate the industry's biggest hurdle—false alarms caused by motion artifacts (e.g., brushing teeth):
We implement **Multi-Sensor Fusion** between ECG and IMU (Accelerometer/Gyroskop).
Rather than just checking if the patient is moving, we exploit **Ballistocardiography (BCG)**. A true heartbeat creates a microscopic, mechanical shockwave. We look for the *phase-locked physiological coupling* between the electrical QRS-complex and the mechanical IMU micro-vibrations. If the EKG shows chaos but the IMU lacks synced BCG waves, it's a motion artifact.

## 📊 ANSI/AAMI EC57 Compliance Matrix

Every model compiled for the MAX78002 must generate an automated validation report containing the following strictly defined metrics across all target test databases (MIT-BIH, AHA, ESC, NST):

| Metric | Formula | Clinical Target (Normal/A-fib) | Clinical Target (Asystole/VF) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Sensitivity ($Se$)** | $TP / (TP + FN)$ | $\ge 98.0\%$ | $\ge 99.5\%$ |
| **Positive Predictivity ($+P$)** | $TP / (TP + FP)$ | $\ge 96.0\%$ | $\ge 99.0\%$ |
| **False Negative Rate ($FNR$)** | $FN / (FN + TP)$ | $\le 2.0\%$ | $\le 0.5\%$ |
| **False Positive Rate ($FPR$)** | $FP / (FP + TN)$ | $\le 1.0\%$ | $\le 0.1\%$ |

*Note: The True Negatives ($TN$) used for $FPR$ are calculated based on the total number of non-event sliding windows or annotated normal beats evaluated by the MAX78002 firmware.*

## 📊 ANSI/AAMI EC57: Atrial Fibrillation (AF) Episode & Duration Matrix

To comply with the strict regulatory requirements of **ANSI/AAMI EC57 (Section 4.2.3)**, the algorithms compiled for the **MAX78002** must be validated on an episodic and duration macro-level, rather than just classifying isolated, individual heartbeats. 

Your validation pipeline must process the time-stamped annotation files (`START_AF` and `END_AF`) generated by the on-chip firmware and compute the following metrics against the gold-standard reference databases (MIT-BIH, AHA):

| Evaluation Level | Performance Metric | Mathematical Formula | Clinical Significance (What are we measuring?) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Episode Level** *(Event-based)* | **AF Episode Sensitivity ($Se$)** | $$Se = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}$$<br><br>*$TP$: An overlap exists between reference and algorithm episodes.*<br>*$FN$: A reference episode exists but was completely missed by the algorithm.* | Measures the chip's ability to **detect the precise onset and termination** of an Atrial Fibrillation attack. |
| | **AF Episode Positive Predictivity ($+P$)** | $$+P = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}$$<br><br>*$FP$: The algorithm flagged an episode that does not exist in the reference data.* | Measures **alarm reliability**. What percentage of triggered AF alarms are genuine vs. false positives caused by motion artifacts or noise? |
| **2. Duration Level** *(Time-based)* | **AF Duration Sensitivity ($Se$)** | $$Se = \frac{\text{Duration of Overlap (sec)}}{\text{Total Duration of Reference AF (sec)}}$$ | Measures how precisely the chip **clocks the exact seconds and minutes** of an ongoing episode. Evaluates window-boundary errors. |
| | **AF Duration Positive Predictivity ($+P$)** | $$+P = \frac{\text{Duration of Overlap (sec)}}{\text{Total Duration of Algorithm AF (sec)}}$$ | Measures the proportion of the total alarmed time that was actually true AF. Critical for calculating an accurate **AF Burden** for clinical reporting. |
| **3. Record Level** *(Holter File-based)* | **AF Record False Negative Rate ($FNR$)** | $$FNR = \frac{AFRecFN}{AFRecFN + AFRecTP}$$<br><br>*$AFRecFN$: A record where reference contains AF, but the algorithm detected **less than 5%** of its total duration.* | Evaluates the risk of **completely failing to diagnose a patient** who actually suffers from paroxysmal or chronic AF during a 24-hour Holter monitoring window. |
| | **AF Record False Positive Rate ($FPR$)** | $$FPR = \frac{FP_{Rec}}{FP_{Rec} + TN_{Rec}}$$<br><br>*$AFRecFP$: A completely healthy reference record (no AF) where the algorithm incorrectly claims AF episodes exist.* | Measures the risk of **falsely diagnosing a healthy patient**, which would trigger unnecessary downstream clinical pathways, anti-coagulant therapies, or neurological referrals. |

### 🛠️ Implementation Guidance for PhD Researchers
1. **On-Chip State Machine (C Firmware):** The Cortex-M4 core on the MAX78002 must track consecutive CNN inference windows. If the residual-CNN flags AF for $X$ consecutive windows, commit a `START_AF` timestamp to the local log. If normal sinus rhythm returns for $Y$ consecutive windows, commit an `END_AF` timestamp.
2. **Offline Validation (Python Skript):** The validation code in this repository must parse these hardware logs and compute the exact overlapping durations (in seconds) matching the `.atr` annotations from the MIT-BIH/AHA datasets according to the exact rules listed in the table above.

### 🧰 The PhysioNet Tool Belt

You will utilize five specific command-line utilities from the PhysioNet suite to generate the authoritative tables required for our scientific reports, peer-reviewed publications, and technical files:

#### 1. `bxb` (Beat-by-Beat Comparison)
* **Purpose:** Evaluates individual heartbeats one by one.
* **Application:** This is the core engine for validating **Project 1's beat classification (slagklassificering)**. It maps out the exact Confusion Matrix comparing whether a beat your model classified as a Ventricular Premature Contraction (VPC/VES) or a normal beat matches the reference expert annotation.

#### 2. `epicmp` (Episode Comparison)
* **Purpose:** Performs macro-level episode and duration tracking over continuous time series data.
* **Application:** Crucial for our **Atrial Fibrillation (AF) and Cardiac Arrest (Asystole/VF) detection**. `epicmp` executes the precise overlapping math required by Section 4.2.3 of the EC57 standard to calculate:
  * **AF Episode $Se$ and $+P$** (Event-based precision).
  * **AF Duration $Se$ and $+P$** (Time-based precision used to quantify *AF Burden*).

#### 3. `rxr` (Run-by-Run Comparison)
* **Purpose:** Analyzes sequential "runs" or bursts of consecutive abnormal beats (e.g., a short burst of ventricular tachycardia).
* **Application:** Used to fulfill our project requirement of distinguishing between isolated, singular pathological beats and **sustained, chronic arrhythmias**.

#### 4. `mxm` (Measurement-by-Measurement Comparison)
* **Purpose:** Compares continuous clinical measurements derived from the signal.
* **Application:** Used primarily to score and report our Heart Rate Variability (HRV) parameters extracted by the on-chip firmware.

#### 5. `sumstat` (Summary Statistics Generator)
* **Purpose:** Aggregates individual patient records into a unified, statistically rigorous summary sheet.
* **Application:** Once you run `bxb` or `epicmp` across all 48 individual patient records of the MIT-BIH database, you will feed the output files into `sumstat` to generate the final, global performance matrix ($Se$, $+P$, $FNR$, $FPR$) for the entire dataset.
